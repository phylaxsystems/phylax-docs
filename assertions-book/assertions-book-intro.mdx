Use the Book to choose a pattern, understand an exploit mechanism, or run a local exercise:

- **Reference**: a catalog of reusable assertion patterns you can adapt to your own protocol
- **Explanation**: case studies of exploit mechanisms and candidate policies, with enforcement assumptions
- **Tutorial**: a guided local exercise with expected allowed and rejected outcomes

You can adapt these examples to write your own assertions. Work with [Phylax](../credible/work-with-phylax) to agree the policy scope and support included in your engagement. Custom policy development includes reviewing protocol assumptions, legitimate exceptions, and integration coverage; an example is not a ready-made production policy.

Pattern pages identify implementation scope and evidence. Historical case studies explain candidate policies and their limitations.

Full examples and mock protocol code are available in [credible-std](https://github.com/phylaxsystems/credible-std/tree/fe49de6dcddb8ea1f77ea2828a0c9d7d5f923db0/examples/assertions-book).

## Ethereum Deployment Scope

The examples demonstrate assertion logic, not complete production integrations. On Ethereum, protection also requires activation at participating builders, an unavoidable guard on each protected path, and transaction routing. Exploit replays establish detection under their test conditions, not universal protection. See [Ethereum Integration Planning](../credible/dapp-integration).

Historical examples retain the networks on which the incidents occurred. Sequencer infrastructure designs live separately in [L2 and Sequencer Integrations](../credible/l2-integrations/overview).
