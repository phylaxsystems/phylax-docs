## Find the Right Reference

The **Patterns** sidebar groups entries by risk: flow limits, authority and configuration, accounting and operation results, solvency, and oracle valuation. Legacy entries remain in their risk group and state the corrections they need. Read the [catalog facets](./assertions/use-cases-index#read-the-catalog-facets) to distinguish risk from protocol family, dispatch mechanism, specification, lifecycle, and evidence.

**Protocol-specific Policies** contains the [ERC-4626 composition](./assertions/vault-policies), the [experimental Aave V3 catalog](./assertions/aave-v3-suite), and the [historical Optimism FMA catalog](./assertions/optimism-fma-suite). Their status and coverage differ; read each reference's source and limits before adapting it.

The [reviewed credible-std revision](https://github.com/phylaxsystems/credible-std/tree/fe49de6dcddb8ea1f77ea2828a0c9d7d5f923db0/examples/assertions-book) contains the Book's imported examples and mock contracts. Pages identify separately maintained local fixtures and the tests executed for them.

## Ethereum Deployment Scope

The examples demonstrate assertion logic, not complete production integrations. On Ethereum, protection also requires activation at participating builders, an unavoidable guard on each protected path, and transaction routing. Exploit replays establish detection under their test conditions, not universal protection. See [Ethereum Integration Planning](../credible/dapp-integration).

Historical examples retain the networks on which the incidents occurred. Sequencer infrastructure designs live separately in [L2 and Sequencer Integrations](../credible/l2-integrations/overview).

For your own policy, follow [Write Your Own Policies](../credible/writing-your-own-policies) and agree the scope with [Phylax](../credible/work-with-phylax). Custom development includes reviewing protocol assumptions, legitimate exceptions, and integration coverage.
