This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Oracle and valuation. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Mudit Gupta’s October 28, 2021 analysis describes the prior day’s Cream exploit: leveraged vault-share exposure and a donation changed the vault exchange rate, creating debt beyond the account’s collateral. It reports over $130 million extracted from available lending assets. [Mudit Gupta incident analysis](https://mudit.blog/cream-hack-analysis/).

## Candidate policy

Constrain the exchange rate actually consumed in lending decisions and recompute account solvency across affected markets. Treat donations and recursive borrowing as part of the same economic transition.

## Coverage and limitations

An event is not a substitute for the consumed oracle value. A fixed donation threshold or two direct reads without snapshot selection does not demonstrate prevention. Legitimate yield, fees, and donated assets require defined treatment.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
