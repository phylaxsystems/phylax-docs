This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Authority and configuration. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Drift’s April 16, 2026 recovery update attributes $295,706,374.93 of stolen assets to the April 1 incident, using its incident-time valuation. Its response includes dedicated signing devices, independent transaction-content verification, timelocks, and disabling durable-nonce transactions. These operational controls matter alongside constraints on administrative authority. [Drift recovery update](https://www.drift.trade/updates/incident-recovery-update-april-16-2026-now).

## Candidate policy

For an EVM design with comparable authority, constrain changes to administrators and high-impact actions, and separately bound asset outflow.

## Coverage and limitations

Drift is a Solana incident. This is a threat-model analogy, not a claim that Phylax EVM assertions run on Solana. A policy controlled by the same compromised authority needs an independent update boundary.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
