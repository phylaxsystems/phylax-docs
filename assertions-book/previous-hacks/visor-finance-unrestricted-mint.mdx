This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Solvency and operation results. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

The DeFiHackLabs reproduction supplies an attacker-controlled `from` contract whose `owner` returns the attacker and whose `delegatedTransferERC20` does nothing. It calls RewardsHypervisor’s deposit operation to demonstrate the unbacked-mint path. A successful call to an attacker-controlled transfer implementation is not evidence that VISR arrived in custody. [Pinned DeFiHackLabs reproduction](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs/blob/33de12241db1019e1516f6740fd19c7b5e3b003c/src/test/2021-12/Visor_exp.sol).

## Candidate policy

Compare the actual custody-token balance increase with shares minted at each deposit boundary, using the pre-deposit asset and supply basis and the intended rounding rule.

## Coverage and limitations

An arbitrary callback can return successfully without transferring tokens. Fee-on-transfer assets, rebases, donations, zero supply, and reentrancy require explicit treatment. The external reproduction was inspected, not executed in this review; it contains no Phylax assertion.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](/credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](/credible/architecture-overview).
