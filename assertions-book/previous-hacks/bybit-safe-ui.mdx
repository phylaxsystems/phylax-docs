This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Authority and configuration. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Bybit’s February 26, 2025 forensic update reports compromise of Safe’s environment and manipulation of the signing flow; it reports no compromise of Bybit infrastructure. The incident illustrates why a valid signature does not establish that the signer saw the transaction’s actual effect. [Bybit forensic update](https://www.bybit.com/en/press/post/bybit-confirms-security-integrity-amid-safe-wallet-incident-no-compromise-in-infrastructure-blt9986889e919da8d2).

## Candidate policy

Constrain the actual proxy implementation and relevant Safe configuration at explicit snapshots. Add an independently specified custody outflow policy where needed.

## Coverage and limitations

A transaction-end implementation check misses a temporary implementation change that is restored before the endpoint. Native ETH loss needs a tested balance-read policy; creating fork identifiers without using them is insufficient. Signing-device security and independent review remain separate controls.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
