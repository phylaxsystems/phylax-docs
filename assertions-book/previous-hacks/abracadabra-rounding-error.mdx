This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Accounting and rounding. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

The pinned CauldronV4 source tracks debt using `elastic` amounts and `base` shares. `repayForAll` and the conversion paths must preserve a meaningful relationship between the two. Reducing the debt amount while leaving shares outstanding creates a precision-sensitive state; a ratio computed from that state is not an independent solvency check. [Pinned CauldronV4 source](https://github.com/Abracadabra-money/abracadabra-money-contracts/blob/cb97c71c8fbfecbd0080e0cfa877f2dc37f56950/src/cauldrons/CauldronV4.sol).

## Candidate policy

Validate debt/share transitions, permitted zero states, and post-operation solvency with explicit integer rounding. Treat debt forgiveness, repayment, and borrow as distinct transitions.

## Coverage and limitations

A blanket equality between debt units and shares is incorrect. A nonzero-share/zero-debt rule also needs protocol-specific handling of legitimate debt forgiveness. The pinned version includes a minimum-elastic guard in `repayForAll`; it must not be treated as byte-for-byte evidence of the exploited deployment. This source review supports the accounting discussion, not a verified incident loss total or a pinned replay.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
