This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Oracle and valuation. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

SlowMist’s incident-response recap explains that an inherited forwarder `execute` path allowed calls that passed a trusted-forwarder check without the intended keeper authorization. Manipulated prices were then used to open and close positions. Its revised estimate was $8.44 million, and it reports recovery of the stolen funds; earlier $7.5 million estimates describe an earlier accounting of the incident. [SlowMist recovery recap](https://slowmist.medium.com/slowmist-assists-kiloex-in-recovering-all-stolen-funds-incident-recap-273d10f6d466).

## Candidate policy

Validate the effective keeper authorization and the price consumed when a position is opened or closed. Treat forwarder identity and original-caller authorization as separate properties.

## Coverage and limitations

The [tested oracle snapshot example](../assertions/ass28-intra-tx-oracle-deviation) demonstrates temporary excursions in a synthetic writable oracle. It is not a KiloEx replay and does not establish coverage of the forwarded execution or consumed price. A market deviation threshold requires independent calibration.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](/credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](/credible/architecture-overview).
