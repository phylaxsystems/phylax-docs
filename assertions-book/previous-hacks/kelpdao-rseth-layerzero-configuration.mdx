This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Cross-chain verification. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

LayerZero’s May 18, 2026 incident report describes the April 18 release of 116,500 rsETH after compromise of verifier infrastructure. It describes a social-engineering intrusion, poisoned RPC responses used by the verifier, and disruption of alternative RPCs. A one-verifier configuration made that verifier a critical trust dependency. A configuration-only account omits the infrastructure compromise. [LayerZero incident report](https://layerzero.network/publications/kelpdao-incident-report.pdf).

## Candidate policy

Bound bridge inflow and constrain changes to the configured verifier set. A backing/supply policy would need authenticated source-chain evidence with explicit finality and reconciliation rules.

## Coverage and limitations

An EVM snapshot does not independently establish source-chain truth. If every comparison trusts the compromised verifier, apparent parity can still be false. A flow cap bounds exposure only for covered assets and paths; it does not authenticate messages.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](/credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](/credible/architecture-overview).
