This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Solvency and operation results. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Verichains’ October 2025 analysis describes a `cook` action sequence in which an additional action replaced the accumulated status and cleared the flag requesting a final solvency check. An action sequence such as `[5, 0]` therefore matters for its resulting debt and collateral, not just for whether an individual action looked valid. [Verichains incident analysis](https://blog.verichains.io/p/abracadabra-money-hack-analysis).

## Candidate policy

Require post-transaction solvency for every affected borrower, regardless of the contract’s internal flag. Discover affected accounts from all relevant actions and read independent debt and collateral state.

## Coverage and limitations

A final check on only `msg.sender` can omit beneficiaries or other modified accounts. Liquidation, debt transfers, price changes, and intended temporary insolvency within a transaction require precise boundary selection. The protocol’s own unchecked flag cannot be the sole trigger for the assertion.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
