This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Solvency and operation results. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

CertiK’s analysis describes the March 13, 2023 Euler exploit as combining leveraged positions, `donateToReserves` without a health check, and liquidation mechanics. The reported approximately $197 million exploit amount is distinct from later recovery valuations. [CertiK incident analysis](https://www.certik.com/blog/euler-finance-incident-analysis).

## Candidate policy

Apply a protocol-specific solvency condition after every operation that can reduce backing or increase debt, including donations. Validate liquidation outcomes as a separate operation with its own allowed transitions.

## Coverage and limitations

A generic rule requiring every account to stay healthy would block legitimate liquidations of already unhealthy accounts. Define which account, valuation, boundary, and authorized transition the policy covers. Checking only ordinary borrow calls leaves donation paths uncovered.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
