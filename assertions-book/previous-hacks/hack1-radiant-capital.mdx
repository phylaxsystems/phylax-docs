This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Authority and configuration. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Radiant’s reimbursement proposal identifies the October 16, 2024 multisig exploit as causing over $50 million in losses. The case concerns compromised authorization and subsequent control of protocol assets, rather than a mathematical error in lending accounting. [Radiant reimbursement proposal](https://community.radiant.capital/t/feedback-101-deploying-merged-claim-contracts-for-radiant-depositor-reimbursement/1936).

## Candidate policy

Lock the configured owner and administrator slots outside an authorized change process, and constrain the operations or outflows those roles can initiate.

## Coverage and limitations

A correct signature can authorize a malicious change. The assertion update authority, allowed migrations, proxy layout, and all relevant control slots must be specified. Checking one owner slot does not protect every administrative path.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
