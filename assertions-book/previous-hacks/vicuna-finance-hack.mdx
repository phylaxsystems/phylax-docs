This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Oracle and valuation. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

The investigator’s post-mortem dates the Sonic exploit to March 28, 2025 and describes approximately $700,000 extracted by manipulating the balances used to value Beets LP collateral. Borrowing against the inflated valuation and reversing the manipulation left bad debt. [Vicuna investigator post-mortem](https://d23e.ch/research/vicuna-post-mortem/).

## Candidate policy

Validate the LP valuation consumed by each borrow against a model that accounts for pool composition, invariant, and trustworthy component prices. Check account solvency using that valuation.

## Coverage and limitations

A simple sum of manipulable reserves can reproduce the oracle defect. A fixed percentage change bound is a separate exposure control, not proof of fair value. Restoration of reserves before transaction end does not repair a borrow already priced incorrectly.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
