This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Accounting and rounding. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Akshay Srivastav’s Compound V2 research explains how a very small initial share supply and a direct donation can raise the exchange rate so that a later deposit rounds down severely, potentially to zero shares. This is a vulnerability pattern, not one incident with a single loss total, and it is not a claim about every lending protocol. [Original first-deposit research](https://akshaysrivastav.hashnode.dev/first-deposit-bug-in-compoundv2-and-its-forks).

## Candidate policy

Check the minimum shares returned for a deposit and bound permitted rounding loss using explicit asset and share units. Define bootstrap behavior separately from the mature pool.

## Coverage and limitations

A minimum total supply alone does not validate the depositor’s outcome. Initialization, empty-pool transitions, direct donations, decimal differences, and legitimate exchange-rate changes must be tested. The right baseline depends on the actual share-accounting design.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](/credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](/credible/architecture-overview).
