This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Accounting and rounding. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

QuillAudits’ analysis of the December 2024 incident describes repeated unstaking because `unStake` did not reject an already inactive stake. The issue is reuse of a redeemed stake, not merely bypassing its maturity time. [QuillAudits incident analysis](https://www.quillaudits.com/blog/hack-analysis/vestra-dao-500k-exploit-analysis).

## Candidate policy

At each covered unstake boundary, require the selected stake to be active before the call, inactive afterward, and paid at most its permitted amount. Select the stake using the actual caller and stake identifier.

## Coverage and limitations

Transaction-end inactivity alone passes both the first and a repeated redemption. Nested calls, multiple stakes, legitimate partial exits, and storage layout must be covered. No conclusion about the project’s audit history follows from this bug.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](/credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](/credible/architecture-overview).
