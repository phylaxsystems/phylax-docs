This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Authority and configuration. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

Blockscope’s September 2025 investigation traces a compromised multisig, privileged delegatecalls, and unauthorized token minting. Its loss estimate includes a different scope from early stolen-asset estimates. This page does not add stolen assets and the notional value of minted tokens into a single loss figure. [Blockscope investigation](https://research.blockscope.co/uxlink-exploit-analysis).

## Candidate policy

Constrain the owner set, threshold, modules, fallback handler, and implementation as a configuration graph. Apply separate limits to privileged minting and asset movement.

## Coverage and limitations

A fixed minimum threshold alone permits replacement of all signers. A cooldown must have a trustworthy recorded baseline and upgrade path. A compromised policy administrator may be able to remove the control; that authority belongs in the threat model.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](../../credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](../../credible/architecture-overview).
