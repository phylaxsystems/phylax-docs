This case explains the failure mechanism and the policy boundary it suggests.

**Risk domain:** Accounting and rounding. **Evidence:** source review, November 17, 2026. **Policy maturity:** proposed; no pinned Phylax exploit replay is supplied here.

## What the source establishes

BlockSec’s analysis describes the September 2, 2025 Bunni exploit as repeated small withdrawals compounding a rounding error into an approximately $8.4 million drain. The affected liquidity calculation and subsequent swaps must be considered together; a single small error is not the complete attack mechanism. [BlockSec incident analysis](https://blocksec.com/blog/bunni-incident-repeated-small-withdrawals-compound-a-rounding-error-into-an-8.4m-drain).

## Candidate policy

Check withdrawal accounting against burned shares using the same pre-operation asset and supply basis. Track aggregate error across the batch, with token-specific units and explicit rounding tolerance.

## Coverage and limitations

A fixed per-call tolerance can itself accumulate into a large loss. Idle balances, active liquidity, fees, supply changes, and downstream pricing must be reconciled. The earlier conceptual fragment did not demonstrate that reconciliation and is not retained as executable guidance.

A production claim also requires a pinned implementation, allowed-operation tests, a replay of the relevant failure, and verified trigger and enforcement coverage. A local example passing tests establishes only its stated fixture behavior. See [Testing Strategy](/credible/testing-strategy) and [Ethereum architecture](/credible/architecture-overview).
