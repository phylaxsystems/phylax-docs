title: 'Neutrality & Decentralization'
description: 'Why neutrality is critical to the Credible Layer architecture and how it preserves decentralization for both protocols and networks.'
---

Neutrality concerns who can define policy, change it, and choose which transactions are included. Protocol-authored assertions make policy inspectable, but do not remove operational or administrative trust.

## Protocol Policy and Operator Authority

Protocols define assertions through their authorized management process. The State Oracle’s manager and administrative roles are distinct from platform collaborator permissions. Users need to understand those change paths as well as the rules themselves.

An integrated rollup sequencer applies assertions on the paths it controls. Removing the integration may preserve ordinary rollup operation while removing that enforcement. It does not demonstrate equivalent protection or coverage of forced-inclusion and fallback paths.

On Ethereum, participating builders and authorized markers form an additional trust boundary. Application guards restrict selected actions while active; their bypass, fail-open, and upgrade mechanisms affect access. Removing a builder integration does not instantly remove an application’s guard requirement.

## Availability and Censorship

A protocol policy can reject unwanted behavior, but operators can also omit legitimate transactions. A global credible-block liveness signal does not prevent selective censorship: a builder can keep marking blocks while a particular user’s transaction is omitted.

Assertion data services, policy administrators, registry administrators, and application authorities are control points to assess. Public code and visible registry state help users inspect them; they do not make the system free of centralized dependencies. See [Trust Model](./trust-model).
