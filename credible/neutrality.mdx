title: 'Policy Control and Decentralization'
description: 'Protocol-owned rules, participating Ethereum builders, and the authority added by enforcement.'
---

The Credible Layer separates protocol-authored security rules from Ethereum's consensus rules. Protocols choose assertions through authorized management; participating builders apply those rules during block building, and on-chain guards restrict protected operations to credible blocks while their gate is active.

## Protocol Control

The State Oracle records which manager can change assertion policy for a contract and when lifecycle changes take effect. Rules are inspectable, so users can review the conditions under which protocol activity is permitted.

Management authority can affect availability and protection. A restrictive rule may reject legitimate operations; removing a rule may weaken protection. Public rules and timelocks make these decisions more observable, but do not eliminate their risks.

## Builder Participation

Ethereum builders need not all adopt the Credible Layer. The Credible Registry authorizes the builders whose markers a guard accepts. Those builders are trusted to perform the applicable validation before marking their blocks.

The guard's restriction can reduce the set of blocks in which an operation succeeds. RPC availability and builder participation therefore affect liveness. Fail-open and bypass paths can restore access while reducing mandatory enforcement.

## Administrative and Exit Paths

Review the Protocol Manager, protocol upgrade admins, guard controls, Registry admins, and any guardians separately. Their powers and delays need not be identical. Neither an assertion removal nor a guard removal should be assumed immediate or sufficient to undo every part of an integration.

A recovery or exit path must remain executable under active rules. Test it before enforcement, including lost keys, restrictive assertions, and builder or Registry outages. See [Trust Model](./trust-model).
