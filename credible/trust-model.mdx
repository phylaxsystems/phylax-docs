Ethereum enforcement depends on participating builders, valid block markers, and complete guard coverage. This page defines those assumptions and the recovery tradeoffs.

## Your Control and Phylax's Role

Your protections run in participating builders' infrastructure. The Phylax platform provides the interface and tooling to create and manage them; the builder's Assertion Enforcer performs runtime enforcement.

Protection changes are authorized through onchain contracts. The State Oracle records assertion assignments and lifecycle changes, while the Credible Registry controls builder authorization and credible-block markers. Platform membership and onchain signing authority are separate.

This separation gives you a path to manage protections without the hosted interface. Authorized signers can submit transactions directly to the relevant contracts, subject to the same permissions and delays. It also limits the effect of a platform outage: losing access to the interface does not itself remove active assertions from builders. Builders, their assertion data, chain access, and your integration's guard and routing dependencies must still be available.

### If the Platform Is Compromised

A compromised interface could present a transaction that differs from your intent. For example, it could ask you to authorize an unwanted assertion or remove a protection. Review the destination and decoded transaction data through a trusted signing workflow. A signature authorizes the actual transaction, regardless of what the interface displays.

Recovery depends on the affected authority. The State Oracle's guardian can remove assertions within its permissions, but removal follows the configured delay. It is not an immediate universal off switch. See [State Oracle Administration](./credible-layer-contracts#state-oracle-administration).

Platform compromise can also affect information available to the compromised service or account. The separation of signing authority does not establish a guarantee about customer-data exposure.

### If Administrative Keys Are Compromised

An attacker gains the permissions of the compromised role. State Oracle administration can affect assertion management; Credible Registry administration affects builder authorization. These are separate powers and must be assessed against the deployed contracts. Removing protections can expose a protocol to attacks, while restrictive policies can interrupt its operations.

Timelocks provide a response window for the actions they cover. The Ethereum Credible Registry schedules builder changes, and its guardian can cancel pending operations. Assertion lifecycle changes follow the State Oracle's timing rules. Neither mechanism should be treated as a delay on every administrative or protocol-upgrade action. See [Authority Boundaries](#authority-boundaries).

### What Recovery Can Restore

Recovery aims to restore trusted configuration and enforcement. Depending on the affected component, it can involve removing an unwanted assertion, securing administrative roles, or coordinating a contract migration with builders and protocol operators. A replacement contract alone does not complete recovery: dependent builders and guards must use the intended configuration, and enforcement must be verified again.

Recovery does not reverse finalized transactions or guarantee recovery of losses incurred during an incident. The integration's recovery plan should identify the responsible operators, required permissions, delays, and verification steps. See [Availability and Recovery](#availability-and-recovery) and [Recovery Testing](./testing-strategy#5-rehearse-recovery).

## Enforcement Scope

- **Deterministic checks**: the same assertion, execution context, and state produce the same result.
- **Enforcement before inclusion**: the enforcer reports failures before inclusion; the enforcing integration excludes those candidate transactions.
- **Assertion-defined coverage**: rules protect only the behavior their logic and triggers capture. Passing a rule is not proof of safety against other failures.
- **Configured lifecycle**: the State Oracle determines assertion assignments and activation or removal times.
- **Execution-path coverage**: every protected Ethereum operation must reach the guard, regardless of its submitter or entry point.

Assertions do not replace ordinary chain validity, protocol checks, or finality rules. Their execution is subject to resource limits and the integration's timeout and error policies.

For the separate sequencer inclusion boundary, see [L2 and Sequencer Integrations](./l2-integrations/overview).

## Decentralized Builders

Assertion enforcement is not an Ethereum consensus rule. Protected operations need an unavoidable guard. The Credible RPC routes transactions to participating builders but cannot prevent inclusion by other builders.

The model relies on:

- **Authorized builders** enforcing applicable assertions and marking only blocks they build under that policy.
- **Marker integrity**: the marker is bound to the expected timestamp. The publication design relies on keeping the marker transaction private until the block header is signed and on validators not signing conflicting blocks for the same slot.
- **Registry administration** controlling builder authorization and any upgrade or emergency powers according to the deployed contracts.
- **Complete guard coverage**: protected entry points, internal paths, modules, and alternative execution routes must not bypass the intended gate.
- **Correct guard behavior** for credible blocks, noncredible blocks, Registry errors, fail-open conditions, and authorized bypasses.

A marker is an authorized attestation, not a cryptographic proof that assertions ran. A guard checks the marker rather than independently executing the rules. See [Architecture](./architecture-overview#decentralized-block-building).

## Authority Boundaries

| Role | Responsibility and risk |
| --- | --- |
| Protocol Manager | Controls assertion lifecycle for registered contracts. Compromise can permit restrictive rules or removal of protections, subject to authorization and timelocks. |
| Platform project members | Have the project permissions assigned to them. Platform access is separate from on-chain authority. |
| Protocol owners and upgrade admins | Control protocol changes and any guard installation, replacement, or bypass powers granted by the protocol. |
| Registry admins and guardians | On Ethereum mainnet, admins schedule builder changes and guardians can cancel them. Builder removal is subject to the same delay as additions. See [Registry governance and scheduling delay](./credible-layer-contracts#credible-registry). |
| Transaction submitters | Deliver transactions through the intended route. Submission access does not itself confer assertion-management authority. |

Assertion timelocks provide time to observe and respond to changes. They do not necessarily constrain separate guard, protocol-upgrade, Registry-admin, or guardian powers. Lost keys and compromised roles need a recovery design that remains executable under the active policy.

## Availability and Recovery

A guard can trade mandatory enforcement for availability by failing open after a configured gap in credible blocks. The block-gap condition opens when the gap is strictly greater than the threshold. During fail-open, protected operations can execute in blocks whose builders do not enforce assertions. Existing protocol checks remain in force, and participating builders can continue enforcing assertions.

The Registry also permits [zero-gas-price calls](./credible-layer-contracts#zero-gas-price-calls) without a marker. This supports simulation but also applies to real zero-price transactions where a chain permits them. Simulation success alone is not evidence of enforcement.

Registry-error behavior is implementation-specific. The Safe guard includes fail-open handling for failed or malformed Registry responses; the generic contract guard can propagate a failed Registry read as a revert. Overrides and authorized bypasses can further change behavior.

A block-gap fallback does not necessarily resolve false positives. Builders may keep producing credible blocks while a restrictive assertion rejects a protocol's transactions. Rule updates, removal, or separately authorized recovery may be needed, with their own delays and permissions.

Each integration should define:

- How missing assertion artifacts, enforcement errors, timeouts, and overloaded workers affect inclusion.
- Which operations remain available during degraded service and which protections are relaxed.
- Who can change rules, guards, and recovery settings, and how those changes are monitored.
- How excluded transactions are reported, reconciled, and retried by submitters.
- How recovery works if a key is lost or an active rule rejects the recovery transaction.

## Evidence and Assurance

Review deployed contract paths, active policies, and administrative powers to assess coverage. Public code and registry state alone do not establish it. Project permissions control access to private invalidation data.

Ethereum builders can check the creation data in each State Oracle registration event against its assertion ID. Enforcement during block building reads prepared assertions from the builder's local Assertion Store. Each integration needs a policy for missing or unreadable artifacts.

Local tests, release backtesting, and alerting provide evidence about rule behavior. End-to-end guard, routing, and recovery tests establish different parts of the integration. Audit reports apply to their stated revision and scope, not automatically to later changes or protocol-specific wiring.
