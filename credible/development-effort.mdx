Development effort depends on the policy requirements, existing tests, and required integration changes. Your [FDE](./work-with-phylax) helps scope this work.

## Effort Factors

Assertion authoring effort depends on how well the protocol invariants are defined. The full adoption effort also depends on the network enforcement model and existing contract controls.

### Invariants Defined

Developers familiar with Forge can write assertions in Solidity and test them with `pcl test`. They also need the [snapshot and inspection APIs](./cheatcodes-overview).

### Invariants Not Defined

Define prohibited states, required relationships, and legitimate exceptions before implementation. Use existing engineering and audit work to identify them.

See [Writing Your Own Policies](./writing-your-own-policies).

## Testing Assertions

Testing can take longer than authoring. Existing Forge fixtures can help, but assertions need explicit tests for trigger coverage, snapshot behavior, legitimate exceptions, and failures. Historical backtesting and alerting add evidence without replacing those tests.

See [Testing Assertions](./testing-assertions) for test patterns.

## Integration and Operations

On Ethereum, plan for guard installation or contract upgrades, coverage of alternative paths and wallet modules, and RPC configuration for every transaction submitter. An immutable contract with no usable gate may not support the intended protection.

Review shared components at their deployed revision and review protocol-specific changes separately. Include integration tests, signer coordination, activation delays, false-positive handling, monitoring, and recovery rehearsals in the estimate.

See [Integration Planning](./dapp-integration) for the work to scope before rollout.
