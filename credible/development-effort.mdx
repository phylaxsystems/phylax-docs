Custom policies are developed with [Phylax sales engineering](./work-with-phylax). Your team contributes protocol expertise, reviews legitimate behavior and exceptions, and handles the integration and operational work assigned during scoping. You do not need to author assertions before the first discussion.

## Effort Factors

Assertion authoring effort depends on how well the protocol invariants are defined. The full adoption effort also depends on the network enforcement model and existing contract controls.

### Invariants Defined

When invariants exist, writing assertions is similar to writing Forge invariant tests:

- **Workflow**: Write Solidity, test with `pcl test`(fork of `forge`), deploy via the [platform](https://ethereum.phylax.systems)
- **Learning curve**: A small set of [cheatcodes](/credible/cheatcodes-overview) for snapshot reads (`PhEvm.ForkId`, `ph.loadStateAt`, etc.)
- **Familiarity**: Developers who write Forge tests can write assertions

### Invariants Not Defined

You can write assertions without pre-defined invariants, but having them makes the process easier, more structured, and lowers the risk of overlooking attack vectors.

Defining invariants involves identifying:

- States that should never occur
- Relationships that must always hold (e.g., total supply = sum of balances)

Work with sales engineering to refine these requirements, using existing engineering, security, and audit work where available. For guidance, see [Intro to Invariants ↗](https://jstcz.eth.link/blog/2025/08/13/intro-to-invariants/).

## Testing Assertions

Testing can take longer than authoring. Existing Forge fixtures can help, but assertions need explicit tests for trigger coverage, snapshot behavior, legitimate exceptions, and failures. Historical backtesting and staging add evidence without replacing those tests.

See [Testing Assertions](/credible/testing-assertions) for patterns and best practices.

## Integration and Operations

On Ethereum, plan for guard installation or contract upgrades, coverage of alternative paths and wallet modules, and RPC configuration for every transaction submitter. An immutable contract with no usable gate may not support the intended protection.

Review shared components at their deployed revision and review protocol-specific changes separately. Include integration tests, signer coordination, activation delays, false-positive handling, monitoring, and recovery rehearsals in the estimate.

See [Integration Planning](./dapp-integration) for the work to scope before rollout.
