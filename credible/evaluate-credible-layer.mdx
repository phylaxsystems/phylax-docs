Use this guide to prepare for a technical scoping conversation with Phylax sales engineering. Bring what you already know and record the open questions. You do not need to write Solidity assertions or complete a tutorial first.

## Prerequisites

Identify a technical contact who understands your protocol and the operations you want to protect. If you are still evaluating the model, start with [How Ethereum Enforcement Works](./credible-layer-overview).

## 1. Describe the Protection Need

List the assets and operations in scope, the harmful outcomes you want to prevent, and any known gaps in existing controls. Include examples of normal activity and exceptional operations that must remain possible, such as migrations, large withdrawals, or governance changes.

You can describe these in plain language. Work with sales engineering to turn them into precise policy requirements, including thresholds and exceptions where appropriate.

## 2. Gather Technical Context

Prepare the information available to your team:

- Target networks, contract addresses, source repositories, and relevant documentation.
- Contract architecture, upgrade mechanisms, privileged roles, and external dependencies.
- Transaction submitters, including wallets, multisig executors, keepers, bots, and relayers.
- Existing tests, invariants, audit findings, and examples of legitimate operational activity.
- Known restrictions on contract changes, routing, governance timing, or availability.

Use the agreed private channel for proprietary materials. Credentials and signing keys are not needed for scoping.

## 3. Identify the People and Decisions Needed

Name the people who can explain protocol behavior, approve policy requirements, implement contract or routing changes, authorize lifecycle actions, and respond to incidents. One person may hold several roles; their permissions remain distinct.

With Phylax, agree on implementation and review responsibilities, the initial coverage, and the evidence required before activation. Record unresolved questions instead of treating assumptions as approved behavior.

## 4. Review Ethereum Integration Feasibility

Use the [technical integration plan](./dapp-integration) with sales engineering to assess guard coverage, supported deployments, routing, authority, and recovery. A protected operation that can bypass the required gate needs a resolved integration change or an explicitly narrower coverage claim.

An immutable contract without a suitable gate cannot gain Ethereum protection simply by registering assertions. Review fail-open behavior, legitimate exceptions, and blocked recovery paths before deciding on a rollout.

## Ready for the Discussion

You are ready to start when you can explain the protection need, identify a technical contact, and share the context available so far. Use [Work with Phylax](./work-with-phylax#start-a-conversation) to start the conversation and understand the next stages.

Ethereum infrastructure operators should use [Builder Integration Planning](./ethereum-builder-integration). Sequencer-based integrations have a separate [L2 guide](./l2-integrations/overview).
