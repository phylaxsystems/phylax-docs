Phylax helps DeFi protocols enforce custom security policies before settlement. Policies are implemented as Solidity assertions, checked by participating builders, and paired with guards on protected operations. These docs explain the Ethereum enforcement model; [L2 integration planning](./l2-integrations/overview) describes deployment-specific requirements.

In these docs, a **policy** is the intended rule, a **circuit breaker** is the protection, and an **assertion** is its executable check. A **cumulative-flow breaker** is the rolling-window subtype. See the [terminology reference](./glossary#terminology-across-the-docs) for the related component and lifecycle names.

You can write assertions yourself or work with **Phylax sales engineering** to select policies and plan integration. Agree the support scope with your team. Bring existing assertion code or start with a discussion of your protection needs.

## What You Can Protect

Policies can constrain asset outflows, administrative changes, collateralization, oracle deviations, and relationships between balances or positions. The appropriate rules depend on your protocol and its normal operations.

For example, a rule that rejects every ownership change would also reject a planned transfer to a new governance contract. Policy design must account for legitimate exceptions as well as harmful behavior. The [Use Cases Book](../assertions-book/assertions-book-intro) describes patterns and their limits.

## How Ethereum Enforcement Works

Builders that agree to run the [Assertion Enforcer](./assertion-enforcer) evaluate candidate transactions against your active assertions and exclude violating transactions from the blocks they build. Your team defines and approves the rules through the configured Protocol Manager and signer set. The deployed contracts also define governance, operator, and guardian powers, including manager revocation or assertion removal; review those [administrative boundaries](./credible-layer-contracts#state-oracle-administration) before activation.

Once you protect functions with Phylax circuit breakers, an onchain guard checks that each protected call executes in a credible block. Calls included in a non-participating builder's block revert while the guard's enforcement gate is active.

Protection depends on the agreed policies, covered execution paths, deployed guards, and operational configuration. Learn about [Ethereum enforcement](./credible-layer-overview) and its [trust and recovery assumptions](./trust-model).

## From Evaluation to Operation

Our forward deployed engineers (FDEs) work closely with your team to define, implement, and thoroughly test your circuit breakers. We study your protocol and existing mitigations, then work through the failure scenarios, legitimate exceptions, and thresholds that matter to you.

Within the agreed integration scope, we write assertions, prepare contract and transaction-routing changes for your review, and test a replica of your protocol on a fork of Ethereum. We run backtests, test legitimate and violating transactions, investigate false positives, and rehearse recovery.

Your team reviews the policies and evidence, approves protocol changes, and controls production signing. We prepare and simulate activation transactions for your signers to authorize and execute, then verify the live protection. Monitoring, incident response, and ongoing policy changes follow the operating plan agreed with your team.

Use [Prepare for Your Integration](./evaluate-credible-layer) to gather the information for that discussion. The [Enforcement FAQ](./faq) covers builder behavior, routing, transaction rejection, and recovery.
